Imagine you are the CFO. You have been asked to cut GPU spend without slowing research. You need an answer in thirty seconds, but the team implementing it needs proof. Our signal is 83 percent of allocated compute not appearing in completed work, across 74,849 jobs and 594,004 measured GPU-hours. At a reference price of two dollars and fifty cents, that is 1.485 million dollars of usage. But that signal is not a savings claim. The analysis starts at physical GPU grain, applies strict predicates for outcome, utilization, attempts, walltime, and record completeness, caps duration by job walltime, and deduplicates whole jobs. Recovery is modeled as candidate capacity multiplied by an explicit scenario fraction. On the frontend, the same decision is available in an Executive View, a Facility Tour, an Evidence Drawer, and a read-only AI layer.

The result is two bounded pilots. Platform Engineering gets 463 completed, zero-compute jobs that can be checked for CPU migration. Research Platform Operations gets 1,794 long, low-activity sessions for a notified, grace-period reclaim. The actions overlap on 109 jobs, so the portfolio assigns each job once. That yields 2,148 unique jobs, with a scenario range of zero to 56,586 GPU-hours and a reference-price equivalent of zero to 141,464 dollars. Even the high case is only 9.53 percent of the historical sample, below the 20 percent comparison. We do not overclaim. We start reversible, inspect the evidence, and scale only after the pilot measures the outcome.
