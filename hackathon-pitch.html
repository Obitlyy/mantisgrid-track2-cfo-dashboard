The headline signal is striking: 83 percent of allocated compute did not appear in completed work. But cancelled or low-utilization work is not automatically waste. Treating the whole 83 percent as savings would be an expensive mistake.

Measured baseline and modeled recovery stay separate03 / 06
The analysis begins at the physical GPU record, not an average utilization dashboard. We apply strict predicates for outcome, SM activity, attempts, walltime, and evidence completeness. Candidate duration is capped so a device cannot contribute more time than its job actually ran. Recovery is then modeled as a scenario fraction, with a zero low bound because no savings have been observed yet. Finally, whole-job allocation removes overlap between actions. Missing rerun, delay, CPU, or billing inputs remain unknown rather than quietly becoming zero.

We found two bounded pilots. First, Platform Engineering can move 463 completed jobs with consistently zero SM compute to CPU, after output and queue-capacity checks. Second, Research Platform Operations can reclaim 1,794 long, low-activity sessions after notice and a grace period. The actions overlap on 109 jobs, so the portfolio assigns each job once. Requeued jobs are excluded from recoverable capacity. Both pilots include stop conditions and rollback to the original queue and resource request.

The portfolio covers 2,148 unique jobs. Its scenario range reaches 56,586 GPU-hours, or about 141,000 dollars at the reference price, with a point scenario of 70,732 dollars. Even the high case is only 9.53 percent of historical sample hours, below the 20 percent comparison. We do not hide that gap. That honesty is the point. We give leaders a reversible first move, give operators the evidence, and scale only after the pilot measures the real outcome. Thank you.
